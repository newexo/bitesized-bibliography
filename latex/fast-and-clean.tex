\section{The cold start}

\begin{frame}{The cold start}
    An opportunity in a domain you do not know, from people with no reason to
    trust you.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The old arithmetic: weeks of unpaid work before anyone believes you.
        \item So you bluff, or you decline, or you over-invest in a stranger.
        \item \textbf{That arithmetic changed} --- not because the model does the
              work, but because you can organise the work differently.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%% ===================================================================
\section{The technology}

\begin{frame}{I do not mean a chat box}
    Not a website you paste a question into.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item An agent that runs \textbf{on your own machine}: reads your files,
              writes files, runs commands, reads what those commands printed.
        \item It can act, look at the result, and act again --- with nobody in
              the loop between steps.
        \item Claude Code, Codex, and others. As a category, about two years old.
        \item If you have only used a chat interface, \textbf{this is a different
              thing}, and most of what you have read about prompting does not apply.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why it changed how I think about work}
    The whole filesystem is available context.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Your repositories, your notes, last week's downloads, a meeting
              transcript you forgot about.
        \item \textbf{It does not need to be told what is relevant.} Work that
              took days takes an afternoon. Understating that would be dishonest.
        \item It is also, word for word, the exposure.
        \item \emph{Seeing everything you have} is one fact with two consequences.
              Everything else in this talk follows from that sentence.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{The thesis}

\begin{frame}{Reasoning improved. The core business is still transformation.}
    Models reason better every year. That raises the ceiling on each step.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item It does not change what the machine is: a thing that turns one
              representation into another.
        \item My own slide, written months ago: ``best understood as
              transformation, not reasoning or understanding.''
        \item Better reasoning makes each transformation bigger.
              \textbf{It does not remove the chain.}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{So the job moved}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Not the reasoner. Not the typist.
        \item \textbf{The person who decides where the cuts go.}
        \item Three years ago the skill was prompting. Now it is decomposition.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Code used to be the asset. Now context is.}
    The inversion that makes all of this possible.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Code was expensive and hard to replace. Documentation
              \emph{described} it, and went stale.
        \item \textbf{That has inverted.} Specification, vocabulary and decision
              records now \emph{determine} the code.
        \item Curated context, and an agent rewrites a codebase in hours.
              Code becomes a build artifact.
        \item Which is why developers started thinking like architects.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One engagement, five transformations}
    research $\rightarrow$ planning $\rightarrow$ data $\rightarrow$ code
    $\rightarrow$ hand-over
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Separate agent per stage. Their material does not cross. The last
              stage's \emph{framing} does not cross. Context narrows.
        \item Roles follow for free: \textbf{rung} and \textbf{processor}.
        \item \textbf{Every boundary needs an artifact} --- which is also the
              audit record.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Stage 1: research}

\begin{frame}{Tuesday, week one. An email from Duddly's Duds.}
    A mid-market clothing chain with an unfortunate name and a real problem. They
    want better recommendations than they have. \textbf{Duds} from here on.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item You know recommender systems. You do not know fashion retail.
        \item Nothing is signed. Nobody has paid you.
        \item \textbf{The engagement has already begun.} From this email, anything
              their competitor should not see is already theirs.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The prompt I actually send}
    \begin{quote}
        A recommender for a clothing retailer, strongly seasonal demand.
        \medskip

        \emph{I already know:} Hu/Koren/Volinsky, Koren, SASRec, two-tower.
        \medskip

        \emph{I want:} where calendar seasonality is first-class, not ordering or decay.
        \medskip

        \emph{Surveys first}, reputable venues, then fan out. Five areas, named.
        \medskip

        \emph{Flag anything you are not confident exists.}
    \end{quote}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The seeds and the areas are my judgement. That is not delegated.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What it found, and what it nearly cost me}
    Clothing is seasonal. The research models time as \emph{order} or
    \emph{drift}. Neither says ``week 47 looks like this for everybody.''
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{957 references checked.} The two literatures cite each other
              essentially zero times.
        \item \textbf{The perfect counterexample was withdrawn.} Unchecked, my
              strongest claim rests on a retracted paper.
        \item Same afternoon I scan their competitors. Reading list is
              \textbf{study}. The scan is \textbf{theirs}.
              \emph{Same tool, same day, two owners.}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Stage 2: planning}

\begin{frame}{Thursday, week three. Two hours in a room.}
    Their sponsor, a brand manager, you, and one colleague. You are running an
    \textbf{event storm}: getting the domain out of their heads and into events,
    before anyone designs anything.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item No vocabulary agreed. No scope. No model of anything.
        \item This is where most projects are decided, and almost nothing is
              written down.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Do not ask it to design. Ask it to interview.}
    \begin{quote}\small
        We are conducting an event storm on the topic of time-aware recommender
        systems in clothing retail.
    \end{quote}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item That is the entire prompt. A skill takes over and the agent becomes
              the facilitator: past-tense business events, no implementation, park
              what is uncertain.
        \item The tooling \textbf{refuses to invent}: a command with no trigger
              event is blocked.
        \item \textbf{The prompt got shorter because the method got packaged.}
              The work moved into a reusable definition.
        \item \textbf{The danger here is not error. It is fluency.} Asked to
              design, a model produces a coherent domain that is not yours.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The record keeps the disagreement}
    Every line is marked:
    \begin{quote}\small
        \textbf{[owner]} is the owner's statement. Anything marked
        \textbf{[moderator]} is a question or an observation for the owner to
        confirm, \textbf{and is not a fact.}
    \end{quote}
    \begin{quote}\small
        \textbf{[owner]} One raw store per engagement [\ldots] there is no global
        raw store. \emph{(The moderator first recorded this as a single global raw
        store; the owner corrected it.)}
    \end{quote}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The assistant got the domain wrong and the correction survives.
        \item \textbf{That is the artifact --- not the agreement, the disagreement.}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Stage 3: data}

\begin{frame}{Monday, week four. The extract arrives.}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 31 million transactions. 1.4 million customers. 100{,}000 products.
              Two years.
        \item No documentation. Nobody at Duds can tell you how it was assembled.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{``Profile it. Do not clean it.''}
    \emph{Illustrative, not verbatim --- in practice several paragraphs carrying
    enough of the domain to be useful.}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Report what is wrong. Propose cleaning as a \textbf{list I approve}.
        \item The finding that matters is about \textbf{evaluation, not cleaning}:
              with seasonal data a random split leaks the future.
        \item \textbf{The danger here is tidiness.} A model that cleans unasked
              hands you a clean dataset and a wrong answer.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Their data, and the thing that moved}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Which model may see this is \textbf{decided per store and fails
              closed} --- sensitive rows go to a local model, never into a session
              transcript, never to a hosted service.
        \item And data moves. Re-running a three-year-old experiment gave a result
              \textbf{10.6\% different} --- not a better model, a regenerated
              sample nobody recorded.
        \item \textbf{Identity is a digest, not a filename.}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

%% ===================================================================
\section{Stage 4: code}

\begin{frame}{Week six. Build something they can look at.}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item This is the stage everyone thinks the talk is about.
        \item If the first three stages went well, it is the shortest part of the work.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Tests first, because wrong code does not crash}
    In data science a mistake returns \textbf{a number}, not an error.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Illustrative, not verbatim.} Characterization tests against
              current behaviour, then one change, its failing test, the command,
              the expected output.
        \item I write or approve the tests. The assistant does not grade its own work.
        \item \textbf{When the upstream stages went well this stage is boring, and
              boring is the whole objective.}
        \item Code declares where it came from exactly as documentation does.
              Both are deliverables.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Stage 5: hand-over}

\begin{frame}{Week ten. It works. Now you leave.}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The awkward part --- and the part AI assistance makes materially
              worse if you are careless.
        \item \emph{Illustrative, not verbatim.} Write the decision record now:
              the reasoning available at the time, including the assumption nobody
              tested and the thing you chose \emph{not} to do.
        \item \textbf{Do not revise an executed decision to match what was built.}
              That fabricates foresight. Supersede it instead.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The deliverable is no longer where the value is}
    Your client contracts for a deliverable. They still do, and they are right to.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The brief, the vocabulary, the decision records:
              \textbf{that is what would let anyone rebuild it.}
        \item The contract addresses the deliverable. It says nothing about the
              thing that now matters. \textbf{The industry has not caught up.}
        \item \emph{And yes, this contradicts what I said two slides ago.}
              Nobody has drawn that line.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Whose directory is this?}
    A careless practitioner runs three engagements from one machine, one set of
    notes, one assistant.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A brief conditioned on client A. A glossary derived from client B's
              vocabulary. A specification informed by both. Transcripts that
              touched all three.
        \item \textbf{Several clients could each make a legitimate claim on parts
              of that directory, and nobody could untangle which.}
        \item Not because anyone did anything wrong. Because the value moved
              somewhere the contracts do not describe.
        \item \textbf{This is what provenance is for.} Not to settle the question.
              To make sure you can still answer it when it is asked.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{The record}

\begin{frame}{Five stages, five sessions, five documents}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Every other AI-risk talk warns that the model will leak your data.
        \item The likelier problem: you are \textbf{authoring} a record.
        \item Timestamped, ordered, and more revealing than any deliverable ---
              because it shows how you thought, and beside what.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The ask is commercial, not legal}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Not a lawsuit. ``Send us everything you used,'' or a vendor
              questionnaire.
        \item \textbf{The rule has not been written.} I will not tell you what the
              law says. I will tell you what a client concludes.
        \item Not liability --- the deal, and the referral behind it.
        \item \textbf{Would you be comfortable if Duds read your session history?}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One client per session, by construction}
    Not by remembering. The actual file my assistant reads before it does anything:
    \begin{quote}\small
        \textbf{One project per session.} Directories are separate projects
        [\ldots]. Stay in the one you were asked about.

        Do not open \texttt{student-context}, any engagement material, or
        \texttt{llm-prompts}.
    \end{quote}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A rule that constrains the assistant, not the human.
        \item I have not opened those directories today, and the reason is four
              lines of a text file rather than my memory.
        \item Provenance does not hide the transcript. It makes it boring.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Close}

\begin{frame}{Every failure in this talk produced something plausible}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A believable empty search result. A tidy dataset. A metric. A branch
              name. A session history that looks like ordinary work.
        \item Yesterday I ran a documented command against a half-finished
              refactor. It did not fail. It wrote a confident wrong answer:
              565 missing where there were none.
        \item \textbf{Nothing announces itself. That is what the chain is for.}
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Fast and clean are one discipline}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Two months old, in use, increasingly tested, \textbf{not finished}
              --- and a framework that looks finished and is not is worse than one
              that shows its gaps.
        \item If context is the asset, \textbf{contaminated context is not a
              compliance problem. It is the destruction of the only durable thing
              you own.}
        \item Tomorrow you pitch. What earns the work is not the demo. It is being
              someone who can be let near their data.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Module: where this came from}

\begin{frame}{An assumption nobody had tested}
    A three-year-old belief that a pipeline ``required a three-table join that
    never worked well.''
    \begin{itemize}
        \item It was not a join. It was a dictionary lookup inside a per-customer loop.
        \item Neither author would vouch for the details. The only quantitative
              record was results committed into config files.
        \item Recorded as recollection, explicitly, not as evidence.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{An artifact that impersonated a decision}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A branch named for an architectural alternative, with the dependency
              to match.
        \item ``Never rejected on evidence, because it was never proposed on evidence.''
        \item A name, a dependency and a framing implied a decision that never happened.
        \item Re-running a three-year-old experiment: 10.6\% different. A
              regenerated sample, not a better model.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The record and the reality drifted, and nobody could tell}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Third-party images reached public history as base64 inside notebook
              outputs. The ignore rule for the image directory had been correct
              throughout.
        \item \textbf{The leak did not pass through a tracked directory.}
        \item The framework is not architecture. It is scar tissue.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Module: what counting found}

\begin{frame}{Nobody had ever counted}
    Rules stated in four places that disagree. A policy still in draft.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Five stores, eight categories, repairs: none.
        \item ``A count that changes what it counts is not a measurement.''
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{554 declarations, zero disagreements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Where a document declares a value, the catalogue agrees. Every
              mismatch found was staleness, not divergence of meaning.
        \item \textbf{But 199 lost attributions.} A directory declares a named
              collaborator and joint work; a document in that tree writes its own
              block and inherits none of it.
        \item An attribution obligation destroyed by being \emph{more specific}.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Lineage as written is unusable}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 175 references name no file at all. Three base conventions coexist.
        \item 132 match a filename somewhere --- \textbf{a candidate, not a resolution.}
        \item The measurement found a defect in itself, corrected it, and said so.
        \item Three rulings came out of these numbers. Measurement changed the design.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Module: what the system refuses}

\begin{frame}{It does not infer ownership}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item A model may guess which initiative a document concerns.
        \item It may not decide which engagement it belongs to, or who owns it.
        \item Those come from where the file is, and from a person.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{It does not judge rights, and does not forget}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Fair use, citation and plagiarism are human judgements.
        \item \textbf{The value of the record is knowing when the judgement is hard.}
        \item A destroyed or unreachable ancestor keeps its reference and says so.
              A record that quietly dropped them would be easier to read and worth
              nothing.
        \item Automate the tedious and falsifiable. Reserve the judgement.
    \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
